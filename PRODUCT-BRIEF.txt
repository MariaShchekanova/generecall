GENERECALL — PRODUCT BRIEF
Prepared for the teammate handoff · October 17, 2026

ONE SENTENCE

GeneRecall helps one person turn an existing genetic report into a clear,
source-backed follow-up request to their original testing laboratory.

THE NEED

A person may have a report they do not fully understand, an uncertain result,
and no simple way to organize a useful follow-up. Looking at a public variant
record raises practical questions: Is this the same variant? What does the
public record say? What should I ask the laboratory that issued my report?

Our product hypothesis is that gathering those details and drafting the
request takes enough effort that people postpone it. GeneRecall reduces that
administrative effort while leaving interpretation with the laboratory and
appropriate genetics professionals. This hypothesis still needs user research;
we have not demonstrated clinical benefit or measured time saved.

THE FIRST USER

An adult with an existing genetic test report who wants help preparing a
follow-up. They can provide a short excerpt and confirm one exact variant.
They want a clear next step rather than a database they must learn from scratch.

Example persona: Alex has an old report with a variant of uncertain significance.
Alex wants to ask the issuing laboratory whether an updated review is available,
but needs help collecting the identifier, original label, source, and questions.

The current MVP works best with one variant, a labeled text excerpt, and an
exact ClinVar variation identifier. It is a hackathon prototype, not a validated
clinical service or a substitute for laboratory interpretation.

THE SOLUTION

1. Bring a report excerpt. Paste text or open a .txt file.
2. Read and confirm. Optional AI extracts fields; a conservative evidence parser
   is available. The person can edit the fields and must confirm the exact ID.
3. Compare the sources. Fetch the exact public ClinVar record and show its
   aggregate label beside the report's label, with provenance and review status.
4. Prepare the follow-up. Generate an editable administrative request with the
   relevant details, source links, and questions.
5. Take it away. Copy, download, or print the draft. Nothing is sent automatically.

THE VALUE

The outcome is a prepared conversation: the report's details, the public source,
and a useful draft in one place. The product does the extraction, lookup, evidence
organization, and drafting work for an individual. The individual checks the
information and decides what to do with the draft.

The design priorities are an obvious next step, visible sources, editable
information, and honest handling of uncertainty. A difference between labels is
presented as a question for professional review. A matching label is also not an
assurance that a report is current or that a result has a particular health meaning.

WHAT EXISTS TODAY

• A responsive three-step web workspace: Your report → Review → Your follow-up.
• Pasted text and .txt import, with a 40,000-character report limit.
• Optional OpenAI extraction with a strict response schema and source checks.
• A visibly labeled rules-based fallback when AI is unavailable.
• Editable report fields and an exact-identifier confirmation step.
• Live exact-record lookup from NCBI ClinVar.
• A dated, real public demo snapshot for a dependable demonstration.
• Explicit handling of differences, matching labels, conflicts, and missing data.
• An editable letter with copy, text download, print, and evidence export.
• A local, dependency-free Node application and a deployable Worker build.
• Automated engine and route tests, plus a recorded narrated product demo.

The app has no account system, report database, saved history, scheduled monitoring,
automatic reclassification alerts, PDF extraction, or automatic email delivery.
Exa/deep research informed the original idea exploration. Exa is not a runtime
integration in this build. Do not present it as powering the live evidence lookup.

THE DEMO'S EXACT PREMISE

The old report is FICTIONAL. Its uncertain-significance label is invented for
the demonstration. The public ClinVar record is real: variation 65533, accession
VCV000065533, for the LYST variant shown in the app. A real dated snapshot ships
with the source, and Refresh evidence can request the current record from NCBI.

This demonstrates a difference between a fictional report and a public aggregate.
It does not demonstrate a verified historical reclassification, a real patient's
result, or a change made by the original laboratory. The demo letter must not be
sent as a real patient request. A live lookup and a snapshot are visibly labeled.

Public demo source: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/clinvar/variation/65533/

WHAT WE CAN SAY IN A PITCH

“GeneRecall turns a genetic report into a prepared follow-up. You confirm the
variant, review the public evidence, and leave with a draft for your laboratory.”

Avoid claims that the tool diagnoses, calculates personal risk, independently
reinterprets a variant, detects a proven change in a laboratory's interpretation,
monitors reports in the background, or improves clinical outcomes. Those are not
capabilities or validated results of this prototype.

60-SECOND DEMO SCRIPT

0–10 seconds: “An old genetic report can leave you with a practical question:
what should I ask the laboratory next? GeneRecall organizes that follow-up.”

10–20 seconds: Click Open demo case. “This prior report is fictional. The public
evidence is real. We start with one exact variant, so the comparison is specific.”

20–35 seconds: Show Review and Refresh evidence. “Here are the two source labels,
the public record, and its review information. A difference gives us a question;
it does not prove that the issuing laboratory changed the result.”

35–50 seconds: Click Open my follow-up and edit a line. “GeneRecall prepares the
request with the identifiers, sources, and questions already included.”

50–60 seconds: Download or print. “Review it, make it yours, and bring it to the
right professional. Your next conversation is already prepared.”

WHAT TO VALIDATE NEXT

Ask people with prior reports and genetics professionals to walk through the
prototype using synthetic or appropriately consented examples. Can they confirm
the correct variant? Do they understand what the two labels do and do not show?
Is the draft useful to the receiving laboratory? Where do they hesitate?

Track task completion, corrections needed, identifier errors caught, confusion
about provenance, and draft usefulness. Do not infer clinical efficacy from a
smooth demonstration. Future product choices should follow that evidence.

NEXT PRODUCT PRIORITIES

1. Make the current narrow workflow reliable across more realistic single-variant
   excerpts, ambiguous wording, incomplete records, and provider failures.
2. Evaluate extraction against a labeled, de-identified or synthetic report set.
   Add PDF import only with clear extraction quality checks and user confirmation.
3. Improve source context and matching with genetics-domain review before adding
   broader interpretation features.
4. Choose a privacy and hosting model before introducing accounts, saved reports,
   sharing, or real patient use. Define deletion and access controls explicitly.
5. Investigate laboratory-specific submission instructions and whether a saved
   follow-up history is actually useful. Monitoring is a separate future feature.
